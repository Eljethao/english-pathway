# English Pathway: content review

**Review date:** October 2026
**Scope:** all 144 lessons (12 courses × 12 lessons), 12 timed practice sets, the 20-question placement test, 14 grammar topics and 195 vocabulary items.
**Result:** every lesson **meets the standard**: 17 as written and 127 after fixes. In total **3,727 items were checked and 512 problems were fixed** over two review rounds. No file needed a specialist rewrite. A short list of issues still needs a human specialist (see below).

## What this review is, and what it is not

This is an **expert content review**. It checks the course against public standards, then fixes the problems found. It is **not an official certification or accreditation**:

- Only the test owners can certify exam results: IDP, the British Council and Cambridge (IELTS), and ETS (TOEFL iBT and TOEIC).
- Completing English Pathway does not give a learner an exam score or certificate.
- No course can guarantee a score.

## Standards used

| Area | Standard |
|---|---|
| Level (B1, B2, C1) | Council of Europe, *CEFR Companion Volume* (2020): can-do descriptors for reading, listening, written production and spoken production/interaction |
| Vocabulary level | English Vocabulary Profile (CEFR labels on Cambridge Dictionary) and the Oxford 3000/5000 |
| Grammar level | Common grammar profiles (e.g. English Grammar Profile levels) |
| Exam formats | `src/content/exams.ts`: task types, timings and score scales checked against ielts.org and ets.org in October 2026 |
| Writing and speaking criteria | IELTS public band descriptors (May 2023 version) and the published TOEFL and TOEIC scoring criteria |
| Item quality | Standard item-writing practice: one defensible answer per item, plausible but clearly wrong distractors, no clues from length or wording, True/False/Not Given rigour, all reasonable spellings accepted in gap fills |

## Method

1. Ten reviewers worked in parallel from one written rubric. Each covered six lessons, or a group of the other content files.
2. For every item, the reviewer **solved the question before reading the key**.
3. Reviewers checked:
   - CEFR fit: text length, vocabulary, grammar and task demand.
   - Exam fidelity.
   - Real-world facts. Unverified claims were checked with web searches and corrected or made general.
   - Model answers: word counts and speaking time at 130–160 words per minute.
   - Images: map, plan and picture answers against the SVG source.
4. Reviewers fixed problems directly in the content files.
5. Every file then passed the automatic content validator and the type checker.

- **Independent review:** the reviewer of each new lesson was never its writer.

## What kinds of problems were found and fixed

- **Answer keys:** questions with two defensible answers (for example a TOEIC Part 6 item, a matching pair in the email lesson, placement item 12), and an ambiguous True/False/Not Given model item. All now have one answer.
- **Gap fills:** correct alternatives were missing (spellings, contractions, synonyms), so learners were marked wrong for right answers. Answers that broke the word limit were removed.
- **Exam accuracy:**
  - An IELTS tip described a format removed in 2020.
  - "Essays under 250 words lose marks" was replaced with ielts.org's wording.
  - Band-descriptor quotes were updated to the May 2023 version.
  - The TOEFL Listen and Repeat and Complete the Words tasks were rebuilt to the 2026 format.
  - The listening practice test was extended to the full 40 questions.
- **Facts in passages:** corrected Ebbinghaus, Keynes, the planning fallacy, Marie Curie's ore, a whale study, bee statistics, countershading, the 1956 container ship and others. Claims that could not be verified were made general.
- **Level fit:** short B2 passages were lengthened. Model answers were trimmed or lengthened to fit their real time limits. 39 vocabulary level tags were corrected.
- **Grammar rules:** six over-simplified rules that were actually wrong were corrected, for example "a few = enough", the past perfect and "mustn't". Two "common mistakes" that were correct English were replaced.
- **Code issues found by reviewers and fixed:**
  - Answers with accents ("café") were marked wrong when typed without them.
  - Heading lists used letters that clashed with paragraph letters (now i, ii, iii as in IELTS).
  - The placement test could place a strong learner at B1 after one slip. Its rule was revised and it now skips listening items when the browser has no audio.

## What still needs a human specialist

1. **Synthetic audio (most important):**
   - Listening and speaking audio is generated by the browser's text-to-speech.
   - Pronunciation, intonation, connected speech, word stress (fifteen/fifty) and some spelled letters depend on the voice.
   - Speaker voices may not match the speakers' gender.
   - **Recommendation:** record human audio for the pronunciation, intonation, listen-and-repeat, numbers-and-spelling and natural-speed lessons first. Each audio section can take a recording file.
2. **Writing and speaking feedback is self-assessed**, with rubrics, model answers and automatic checks. A learner who needs a reliable band estimate should get feedback from a qualified teacher.
3. **Score guides are not calibrated:**
   - The practice-test score tables and the placement test are expert judgement, not statistically calibrated.
   - **Recommendation:** pilot the placement test with 20–30 learners of known level and adjust.
4. **Simplified formats:**
   - TOEFL Complete the Words does not show one box per missing letter.
   - TOEIC Part 2 reply options are shown on screen.
   - Learners can replay audio.
   - Build a Sentence has no extra (distractor) chunks.
   - These make practice slightly easier than the real test.
5. **Judgement calls listed per lesson in the review notes:** for example whether "half past five" fits a two-word limit, or whether informal words should be accepted in an academic-register exercise.

## What learners can realistically expect

Each course (one skill at one level) is **4½ to 8 hours**. A learner studying all four skills at one level does about **20–27 hours**, and the whole site is about **66 hours** of lessons plus 4 hours of timed practice. Cambridge English estimates that moving up **one** CEFR level takes about **200 guided learning hours**. So the site is a substantial part of that journey but not all of it, and it cannot promise a score.

What a learner who completes their level properly (doing the exercises, writing and speaking tasks, and practice tests) can expect:

- **Know every task type** in IELTS, TOEFL iBT (2026 format) and TOEIC, with its timing, so there are no surprises on test day and less time is wasted.
- **Use proven strategies** that reduce avoidable mistakes, for example:
  - True/False/Not Given reasoning.
  - Matching headings by main idea.
  - Predicting answers and spotting distractors and paraphrase in listening.
  - Note-taking.
  - Writing a Task 1 overview.
  - Planning a four-paragraph essay.
  - Structuring a two-minute talk.
  - Hedging and comparing in discussion.
- **Build exam vocabulary and accuracy:** about 1,400 exam words with spaced-repetition flashcards, and 14 key grammar topics with their most common mistakes.
- **Practise under time pressure** with 12 timed sections and full-length practice tests for listening, reading and speaking.

Recommended path: take the placement test, complete your level, do the timed practice tests, then retake the placement test. Before booking an exam, also take the **official** practice tests from IELTS or ETS and read and listen to English every day.
